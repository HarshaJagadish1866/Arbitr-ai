# Termination Clause Standards
## LegalShield AI Legal Reference Library

### Why Termination Clauses Matter

Termination clauses define HOW and WHEN a contract can be ended. A poorly
drafted termination clause can leave you locked in an unfavourable contract
indefinitely, or conversely, allow the other party to fire you without cause
at any moment — potentially destroying expected income.

---

### ✅ Standards for a FAIR Termination Clause

A balanced termination clause includes:

1. **Termination for Cause (Both Parties):** Either party can terminate if the
   other materially breaches the contract, subject to a reasonable cure period
   (typically 15–30 days notice to cure before termination takes effect).

2. **Termination for Convenience (If Included, Must Be Mutual):** If either
   party can terminate without cause, BOTH parties must have this right under
   the same notice terms.

3. **Reasonable Notice Period:** Termination for convenience should require
   30–90 days written notice, giving the other party time to transition.

4. **Payment on Termination:** On termination, the contractor is entitled to
   payment for all work completed up to the termination date, plus any
   reasonable wind-down costs.

5. **Return of Materials:** Clear obligations about what happens to deliverables,
   data, and confidential information on termination.

6. **Survival Clauses:** Clear statement of which provisions survive termination
   (confidentiality, IP rights, payment obligations, limitation of liability).

---

### 🔴 Signs of a CRITICAL Termination Clause

Flag as CRITICAL if:

- **Unilateral Termination for Convenience:** Only the client can terminate
  without cause. The contractor has no such right.
- **Termination Without Payment:** The contract can be terminated and the
  contractor receives payment only for COMPLETED (not in-progress) work.
- **Immediate Termination Right:** Client can terminate immediately with no
  notice period — leaving contractor with instant loss of income.
- **No Cure Period for Breach:** Termination for cause can occur with no
  opportunity to cure the breach.
- **Clawback on Termination:** Client can demand return of fees already paid
  upon termination for cause (even minor cause).

---

### 🟠 Signs of a HIGH-RISK Termination Clause

Flag as HIGH if:

- Notice period is shorter than 14 days.
- Only the client has termination for convenience; contractor must have cause.
- Payment on termination is for "completed deliverables" only (not time worked).
- Termination clause is linked to an unfair non-compete (compound risk).

---

### Legal Standards

- **Common Law Principle:** At common law, contracts for services can generally
  be terminated by reasonable notice. Express terms modify this default.
- **US Employment At-Will:** Does not apply to independent contractor agreements
  — these require contractual termination provisions.
- **UK Employment Rights Act 1996:** Statutory minimum notice periods apply
  to employees but NOT independent contractors.
- **CISG (International Sales):** For international goods contracts, CISG
  Article 26 requires notice of avoidance to be effective.
