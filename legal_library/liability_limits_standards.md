# Limitation of Liability Standards
## LegalShield AI Legal Reference Library

### Purpose of Limitation of Liability Clauses

A limitation of liability (LoL) clause caps the total financial exposure of
one or both parties in the event of a breach, negligence, or other contractual
failure. These clauses are nearly universal in commercial contracts.

---

### ✅ Standards for a FAIR Limitation of Liability Clause

A fair LoL clause includes:

1. **Mutual Caps:** Both parties are subject to the same liability cap.
   If only one party's liability is capped, it is one-sided.

2. **Reasonable Cap Amount:** Typically 1–2x the total fees paid under the
   contract. Caps of 3x or more start to become burdensome.

3. **Consequential Damages Exclusion (Mutual):** Excluding lost profits,
   indirect damages, and consequential damages — but MUTUALLY. If only
   the service provider excludes consequential damages, the client retains
   the right to sue for indirect losses while the contractor cannot.

4. **Carve-outs for Gross Negligence and Wilful Misconduct:** The cap should
   NOT apply to liability arising from gross negligence, wilful misconduct,
   fraud, or death/personal injury. These should remain uncapped.

5. **Carve-out for IP Infringement and Confidentiality Breach:** These are
   typically also excluded from the general cap because potential damages
   may far exceed the contract value.

---

### 🔴 Signs of a CRITICAL Limitation of Liability Clause (for the contractor)

Flag as CRITICAL if:

- **No Limitation at All:** There is no LoL clause — one party faces unlimited
  exposure. This is only CRITICAL if the signer is the party without a cap.
- **One-sided Cap:** Client's liability is capped but contractor's is unlimited.
- **Cap Applies to Client's IP Infringement:** The contractor can cause massive
  IP damages but faces only a small cap — dangerous for the client.
- **Cap Below Cost of Work:** Cap is set so low (e.g., $1,000 for a $50,000
  engagement) that it creates a moral hazard.

---

### 🟠 Signs of a HIGH-RISK LoL Clause

Flag as HIGH if:

- The consequential damages exclusion only protects one party.
- No carve-out for gross negligence or wilful misconduct.
- The LoL clause conflicts with applicable consumer protection statutes.

---

### Legal Standards

- **US Law:** Limitation of liability clauses are generally enforceable between
  commercial parties. Courts may void them if unconscionable.
- **UK Law (UCTA 1977):** B2B limitation clauses must satisfy a reasonableness
  test. Exclusion of negligence liability causing death or personal injury is void.
- **EU Directive 2019/770:** For digital content contracts, certain limitations
  on consumer remedies are prohibited.
