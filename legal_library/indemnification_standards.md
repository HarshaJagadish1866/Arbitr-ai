# Indemnification Clause Standards
## LegalShield AI Legal Reference Library

### What is an Indemnification Clause?

An indemnification clause (also called a "hold harmless" clause) is a contractual
provision where one party agrees to compensate the other party for specified losses,
damages, or legal costs. It is one of the most consequential clauses in any contract.

---

### ✅ Standards for a FAIR Indemnification Clause

A fair indemnification clause should have all of the following characteristics:

1. **Mutual Obligation:** Both parties indemnify each other. Neither party alone
   bears the burden of defending the other.

2. **Scope Limited to Own Acts:** Each party is responsible only for claims arising
   from their OWN negligence, misconduct, or breach — NOT for the other party's acts.

3. **Exclusion for Other Party's Negligence:** The clause explicitly states that
   indemnification does NOT cover situations where the other party's own negligence
   or wilful misconduct caused the claim.

4. **Capped Liability:** The total indemnification exposure is capped — usually at
   the total value of fees paid under the contract, or a multiple thereof.

5. **Insurance Requirement:** Often paired with an insurance clause requiring
   adequate professional liability or general liability insurance.

6. **Notice Requirement:** The indemnified party must promptly notify the
   indemnifying party of any claim to allow for a defence.

---

### 🔴 Signs of a DANGEROUS Indemnification Clause

Flag as CRITICAL if ANY of the following are present:

- **Unilateral:** Only one party (the signing party) must indemnify.
- **Covers Other Party's Negligence:** The signer must indemnify even for harm
  caused by the other party's own negligence or wrongdoing.
- **Unlimited Scope:** "Any and all claims, losses, damages" with no carve-outs.
- **Unlimited Dollar Amount:** No cap on indemnification amount.
- **Broad Third-Party Coverage:** Extends to affiliates, officers, employees,
  agents — massively expanding the potential class of claimants.
- **Pre-litigation Costs:** Requires paying legal costs before any determination
  of liability ("duty to defend" without qualification).

---

### 🟠 Signs of a HIGH-RISK Indemnification Clause

Flag as HIGH if:

- The obligation is unilateral but does NOT cover the other party's own negligence.
- The scope is broad ("arising out of or relating to") rather than limited
  ("directly caused by").
- No notice requirement exists for triggering indemnification.
- Indemnification extends to affiliates or parent companies.

---

### Legal Precedents & Standards

- **Comparative Fault Principle:** Under the Restatement (Third) of Torts,
  indemnification that shifts liability for another's fault is disfavoured.
- **Anti-Indemnity Statutes:** Many US states (TX, CA, IL, etc.) have statutes
  that void indemnification provisions that attempt to hold a party harmless
  for their own negligence in certain contexts (construction, oil & gas).
- **UK Law:** Under the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (UCTA), unreasonable
  indemnification clauses in B2B contracts may be unenforceable.
