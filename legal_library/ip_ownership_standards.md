# Intellectual Property Ownership Standards
## LegalShield AI Legal Reference Library

### The IP Ownership Problem in Contracts

IP ownership clauses determine who legally owns work product created during
a contract. For freelancers, contractors, and employees, this is often the
most financially significant clause — it determines whether you retain the
rights to your own creative and technical work.

---

### ✅ Standards for a FAIR IP Ownership Clause

A balanced IP ownership clause should include:

1. **Scope Limited to Commissioned Work:** Only IP created SPECIFICALLY for
   the client under THIS contract is assigned. Pre-existing IP, background IP,
   and general methodologies remain with the creator.

2. **Explicit Pre-existing IP Carve-out:** The clause explicitly identifies
   and excludes any pre-existing tools, libraries, frameworks, or works
   (sometimes listed in a Schedule).

3. **License (not assignment) for Background IP:** If the contractor uses
   pre-existing work in the deliverable, the client receives a LICENSE to
   use it within the deliverable — they do NOT own it outright.

4. **No IP Beyond Scope:** The assignment only applies to work product
   within the defined scope of work — not any adjacent innovations.

5. **Moral Rights Recognition (UK/EU/AUS):** In jurisdictions with moral rights,
   the creator retains the right to attribution, even if economic rights are assigned.

6. **Consideration:** The IP assignment is in exchange for fair compensation
   (which must be clearly stated).

---

### 🔴 Signs of a CRITICAL IP Clause (for the contractor/creator)

Flag as CRITICAL if ANY of the following:

- **"Work Made for Hire" with No Carve-outs:** All work product, including
  pre-existing IP, is assigned to the client.
- **Future Works Included:** Assignment covers future inventions or works
  conceived during or after the contract period.
- **No Pre-existing IP Protection:** No explicit carve-out for background IP,
  tools, or methodologies the creator brings to the project.
- **"Relating to the Business" Scope:** Assignment covers any IP "relating to
  the client's business" — an extraordinarily broad scope.
- **Perpetual, Irrevocable Assignment:** IP is transferred forever with no
  reversion right even if the client fails to pay.

---

### 🟠 Signs of a HIGH-RISK IP Clause

Flag as HIGH if:

- Assignment covers all IP "created during the term" not just commissioned work.
- No explicit pre-existing IP schedule or carve-out, but limited to the scope.
- Assignment includes derivative works but no license-back provision.
- No compensation explicitly linked to the IP assignment.

---

### Legal Standards

- **US Copyright Act (17 U.S.C. § 101):** "Work made for hire" has a specific
  legal definition. Independent contractors' work only qualifies if it falls
  into 9 statutory categories AND there is a written agreement.
- **UK Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988:** Default rule is that the
  creator owns the IP. Assignment must be in writing and signed.
- **Australia Copyright Act 1968:** Similar to UK — creator owns by default.
- **TRIPS Agreement (WTO):** International standard requiring written IP assignments.
